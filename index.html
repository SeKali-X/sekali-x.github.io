Rear Suspension Geometry

Rear Suspension Geometry

The car in action

I designed suspension geometry, balancing an optimal solution between various suspension characteristics. Using the SolidWorks 3D sketching tool, I set up the control arm points and optimized roll centers, toe, camber, vertical wheel travel, track width, recessional travel, and more at different suspension tunes and heights.

As a result, the new geometry preserved the advantages of the previous version—such as constant speed maintenance over obstacles and a consistent ground contact patch—while reducing excessive body roll and the understeer tendency during mid-cornering. I also resolved the previous issue of one-wheeling in corners by increasing track width and rear suspension stiffness.

All decisions were made with careful consideration of proper clearances, allowable loads on the half-shafts and control arms, and full SAE rule compliance in collaboration with the frame and drivetrain teams.

Rear Suspension Arm Structure

FEA with the max wheel loads

After finalizing the rear suspension geometry, I started designing the rear control arm. The main challenges this year were updated requirements on the frame mounts for the arm and integration of double shock mounts. To solve the problem while keeping weight low, I introduced two 0.036" thickness middle tubes and a bridge tab to maintain proper clearances between the shock and the arm. While finite‑element analysis for the arm is still in progress, I used ANSYS to perform rigid‑body simulations of the rear outboard and calculate joint loads at the shaft and frame points. Additionally, I wrote a Python script to compute the moments created by the arm and estimate the tensile loads on the half‑shaft (shown above).

Research & Development

Corrosion Pit

Scanning-electron microscopy on ThermoFisher Apreo 2S

I studied the difference in corrosion resistance between wrought and additively manufactured stainless steel, focusing on the impact of microstructural differences on corrosion. I used the fluorimetry technique (Jacso FP-8650) to measure the bulk corrosion rates of industry-standard 304 and 316 stainless steels. I received extensive training in running wet lab experiments and working with highly sensitive samples. I also earned qualifications in using scanning-electron microscopy with an integrated X-ray spectroscopy device for qualitative surface and pitting corrosion analysis.
